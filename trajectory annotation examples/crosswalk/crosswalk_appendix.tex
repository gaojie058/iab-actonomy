\section{Crosswalk to Concurrent Behavior Schemes}
\label{app:crosswalk}

A reviewer may reasonably ask whether Act*ONOMY restates
vocabulary that concurrent efforts already provide. To answer this
concretely rather than rhetorically, we cross-walk Act*ONOMY
against the two schemes closest to it in intent: MAST~\cite{cemri2025why},
the multi-agent failure taxonomy, and CoALA~\cite{sumers2024cognitive}, the
cognitive-architecture action space we build on.

We report both directions, because they answer different questions.
\emph{Forward} asks whether Act*ONOMY can express the other
scheme's categories. \emph{Reverse} asks how much of Act*ONOMY's
vocabulary the other scheme can reach. A scheme that is expressible in ours
but does not reach most of ours is subsumed by it.

\paragraph{Relation types.}
A MAST failure mode is not itself a behavior; it is a predicate \emph{over}
behavior, asserting that some behavior is absent, wrong, or repeated. The
forward crosswalk therefore records a relation rather than a bare label:
\textsc{direct} (the mode names a behavior that has a leaf, and the failure is
that leaf being absent or misexecuted), \textsc{composite} (two or more leaves
are needed jointly), \textsc{pattern} (a predicate over the annotated leaf
\emph{sequence}, so it applies to any leaf rather than to a particular one),
and \textsc{gap} (no leaf covers it).

\paragraph{What the crosswalk shows.}
Three results. First, Act*ONOMY is expressive enough to state
MAST's taxonomy: 13 of 14 failure modes are predicates over our leaves, and
seven of those are direct, meaning the failure is simply a named leaf being
absent or misexecuted. FM-3.1 (premature termination) is the pattern our
Case Study~\ref{sec:within-agent} surfaces automatically as
\emph{submit anyway, without verifying}. Second, the reverse does not hold:
MAST's modes reach only 36 of our 120 leaves. All fourteen Evaluate leaves are
reached, which is expected for a taxonomy built around verification failure,
but no Retrieval or Learning leaf is reached at all, and only two of
twenty-two Reasoning leaves. A trajectory can be fully annotated under MAST
and still say nothing about how the agent gathered information, what it
inferred, or what it learned. Third, CoALA contains every leaf but
distinguishes almost none of them: eight units span 120 leaves, so
``compare against gold reference'' and ``provide qualitative judgement''
are the same category to it. Reflection has no CoALA home at all, which is
the empirical basis for our keeping Reflection and Learning apart
(\S\ref{sec:taxonomy-overview}).

\paragraph{The gap, and what we did with it.}
FM-2.1, \emph{conversation reset}, has no Act*ONOMY leaf. Our
nearest codes cover discarding working memory (T1.2.3.1) and sending a peer
message (T3.1.4.3), but neither names restarting a dialogue. We report this
rather than stretch an existing code onto it, and we have filed it through the
extension protocol described in \S\ref{sec:extension} as a v4.3 candidate:
\textsc{Grounding $>$ Interact with other agents $>$ Reset conversation with
peer}. A taxonomy that reported perfect coverage of an independently
constructed scheme would be evidence of overfitting, not of quality; the
useful property is that the gap was locatable and that absorbing it is a
defined operation rather than a new paper.

\paragraph{Scope of this analysis.}
This is an analytical crosswalk performed by the authors over published
category definitions, not an empirical re-annotation of MAST's traces. It
establishes expressibility and resolution, not agreement. Mode definitions are
quoted verbatim from MAST; CoALA assignments follow its stated split of
external grounding versus internal retrieval, reasoning, and learning, with
memory reads counted as retrieval and memory writes as learning per its own
definition. The mapping and the code that computes every number in these
tables are released at \texttt{4\_crosswalk/} in our repository so that the
assignments can be audited and disputed leaf by leaf.
